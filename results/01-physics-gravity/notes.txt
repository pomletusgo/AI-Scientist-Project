# Research Paper Context
Title: A Fading-Scaffold Learning Progression from Heuristic Acceleration to Geodesic Light Deflection via a Removable Effective Refractive Index

## Paper Analysis
Domain: Physics Education
Sub-domain: General Relativity Education / Gravitational Light Deflection
Core Problem: How to enable undergraduate students who have not learned general relativity to quantitatively derive the observed 1.75 arcsec gravitational deflection of light around the Sun without solving the full geodesic equation.
Key Trade-off: Balancing conceptual accessibility for students without a general relativity background against the mathematical rigor and correctness of the full general relativistic calculation; previous simplified approaches either require complex formalisms or yield only half the observed deflection.

## Methodology Summary
The intervention has four components. (1) Temporary scalar bridge representation: after the heuristic acceleration derivation, students compute light bending in a weak-field effective refractive index n(r)=1+2GM/(c^2 r). They solve a Fermat-principle integral of the transverse gradient of n along an undeflected path and recover the same 1.75 arcsec value. This representation is explicitly introduced as removable: the instructor labels it as a useful scalar proxy for the metric time component, not a full theory. (2) Progressive fading of the scalar proxy and recovery of metric components: worked examples are arranged in a completion schedule. In the first example the effective index appears fully; in the second students must derive the index from the Schwarzschild time component g_tt = -(1-2GM/(c^2 r))c^2; in the third the scalar n is retired and the bending integral is rewritten as the integral of the spatial gradient of g_tt, which is then reinterpreted as the geodesic acceleration term -Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} u^alpha u^beta. Heuristic acceleration terms are discarded only after students demonstrate they can map them to metric derivatives; metric elements are restored progressively through partially completed equations. (3) Dynamic representational visualization: an interactive simulation lets students move a slider across three coupled views--(a) heuristic acceleration vectors, (b) a color-mapped effective refractive index with Fermat ray, and (c) a spacetime curvature grid with null geodesic and Christoffel-symbol vector field. The same Sun and impact parameter remain fixed so students see that the same deflection is represented at different levels of mathematical abstraction. (4) Embedded formative checks: after each transition, brief prompts ask students to compare two representations and predict what changes when the scalar proxy is removed; these checks are used to adapt the pace of fading in real time.

## Experiment Plan
We will conduct a multi-institution controlled study with 120 upper-division undergraduate physics majors who have completed multivariable calculus and special relativity but no general relativity. Participants will be randomly assigned after a stratified math pre-test to one of three conditions: (A) the original heuristic acceleration derivation only; (B) a traditional Schwarzschild-metric geodesic derivation; (C) the proposed three-stage fading-scaffold sequence with a temporary effective refractive index. Each condition will use three 90-minute active-learning studio sessions with matched worked examples and practice problems. Data collection includes: (1) a pre-test on mathematical prerequisites and Newtonian light-deflection reasoning; (2) an immediate post-test with quantitative problems (Sun deflection, deflection by a star with different mass/radius) and conceptual items (why the GR value is twice the Newtonian prediction); (3) a 6-week delayed retention test; (4) a transfer test with galaxy-cluster lensing and strong-field qualitative items. Metrics are normalized learning gain, Cohen's d effect sizes, a problem-solving rubric, a custom Gravitational Light Deflection Concept Inventory (GLDI) with reported reliability, and a 9-point cognitive load scale after each stage. Primary comparisons are C vs A (does the scaffold improve conceptual and transfer performance without sacrificing quantitative accuracy?) and C vs B (does the scaffold improve accessibility, self-efficacy, and retention compared with a formal geodesic-only route?). Planned ablations within C include: C-noBridge, which removes the effective refractive index stage; C-noFade, which presents all stages as full worked examples without progressive completion or removal of heuristic terms; and C-noSim, which removes the interactive visualization layer. Expected outcomes: C will show a significant advantage over A on conceptual transfer (d > 0.5) and over B on immediate quantitative accuracy and self-efficacy (d > 0.4); C-noBridge will show weaker conceptual linkage between scalar and metric reasoning; C-noFade will show lower delayed retention and transfer; C-noSim will show reduced engagement but not necessarily lower learning, isolating visual scaffolding as motivational rather than cognitive.
