Domain: materials science

## Methodology Summary
1. Microstructure Encoder and Latent Decomposition: A convolutional encoder processes the input microstructure image (voxel map of phases, orientations) into a set of spatially varying feature maps. These maps are fed to a gating network (a small CNN) that produces soft assignment weights for K experts per pixel, effectively segmenting the domain into learned ‘material motifs’ without human labeling.
2. Mixture-of-Experts Field Decoders: K parallel decoder networks, each specialized on a specific motif, predict the local field components (e.g., stress tensor) from the encoded features and loading condition embedding. The final field prediction is a pixel-wise convex combination of expert outputs weighted by the gating probabilities. This modular design allows different physical behaviors (e.g., plasticity in soft phases, elasticity in stiff phases) to be captured by distinct experts.
3. Self-Supervised Physics Constraints with Adaptive Weighting: The training loss includes a data fidelity term plus a physics penalty derived from the equilibrium condition (divergence of stress = 0) and material rotation compatibility. The penalty coefficient at each spatial location is adaptively scaled during training based on the local prediction variance across the K experts (a measure of epistemic uncertainty). This dynamic scheduling, implemented via an online meta-learning loop, ensures that physics constraints are enforced more stringently in regions of high uncertainty, preventing overfitting to noisy data and improving extrapolation.
4. Training and Stability: We employ a two-phase training: first, warm-up with a fixed loss balance to establish expert specialization; second, introduce adaptive weighting using auxiliary gradient ascent to maximize physics satisfaction while minimizing data error. Expert diversity is encouraged via an additional entropy regularization on the gating outputs.

## Experiment Plan
1. Datasets: We will use synthetic 2D and 3D microstructures generated via phase-field simulations (spinodal decomposition, grain growth) and experimental scans (e.g., serial sectioning of dual-phase steel, ceramic composites). For each microstructure, high-fidelity finite element (FE) simulations under multiple loading conditions (tension, shear, thermal) will generate ground-truth local stress, strain, and damage fields. The dataset will include diverse volume fractions, morphologies, and anisotropy. We will split 80/10/10 for train/validation/test, ensuring test sets include morphology types not seen in training.
2. Metrics: Prediction accuracy via relative L2 error of field variables; physical consistency metrics such as equilibrium residual (divergence of stress), work conjugacy gap, and satisfaction of von Mises criterion; interpretability via attribution maps comparing learned experts to microstructural features; generalization gap between seen and unseen morphologies.
3. Baseline comparisons: (a) Standard CNN encoder-decoder without physics constraints; (b) Single-network physics-informed neural network (PINN) with fixed global loss weights; (c) Homogenization-based approaches (e.g., FE-ANN with Mori-Tanaka); (d) Mixture-of-experts without adaptive loss weighting.
4. Expected outcomes: The proposed model will outperform baselines in field accuracy on unseen morphologies, exhibit lower physical inconsistency metrics, and its expert specialization will align with microstructural phases (grains, boundaries, inclusions). Adaptive physics weighting will improve extrapolation to extreme loading conditions.
5. Ablation studies: Remove adaptive loss scheduling (fixed weights), disable segmentation (single decoder), replace gating mechanism with hard clustering based on grain orientation, and vary the number of experts. Evaluate impact on accuracy, physical violations, and training stability.
