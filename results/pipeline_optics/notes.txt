## Methodology Summary
The proposed method comprises four integrated components. (1) Programmable Compressive Illumination: A DMD coupled to a resonant galvo scanner generates structured sparse illumination patterns updated at kHz rates, enabling flexible undersampling patterns that can be adapted in real time. (2) Online Diffusion Prior Update: A lightweight diffusion model is initialized from a few static super-resolved frames and is continuously fine-tuned using a small buffer of recently reconstructed frames, allowing the prior to adapt to sample photophysics, drift, and morphological changes during imaging without requiring pre-existing training sets. (3) Spatiotemporally Consistent Reconstruction: The compressive measurements are inverted by solving an optimization that includes a fidelity term, a diffusion prior score-matching regularization, and a temporal coherence term that penalizes deviations from motion-compensated previous frames using an optical flow-based warping kernel. This reduces frame-to-frame flicker and preserves identity of moving organelles. (4) Adaptive Sampling Strategy: Local motion magnitude, estimated from fast coarse reconstructions, guides the DMD mask generation: regions of high dynamics receive higher sampling density in the next frame, while static regions are sparsely sampled, maximizing information capture under a fixed photon budget. This online feedback loop runs entirely on a GPU-accelerated pipeline for real-time operation.

## Experiment Plan
We will validate the method on live HeLa cells expressing Lifeact-mNeonGreen (actin) and Rab5-mScarlet (endosomes) imaged at 37°C with a resonant-scanning confocal microscope modified for programmable compressive illumination using a digital micromirror device (DMD). Datasets will include: (1) static nanoparticle phantoms for calibration, (2) live-cell actin dynamics with varying undersampling ratios (4×, 8×, 12×, 16×), (3) endosome trafficking under pharmacological perturbations (Nocodazole, Brefeldin A). Baselines: (a) full STED acquisition (gold standard), (b) compressive sensing with fixed diffusion prior from static training, (c) compressive sensing with non-adaptive online prior (fixed update frequency), (d) total variation regularization. Metrics: spatial resolution by Fourier ring correlation (FRC) between reconstructions and full STED, temporal resolution (effective frame rate), PSNR/SSIM, single-particle tracking measures (localization precision, track length, diffusion coefficient extraction), photobleaching rate (fluorescence decay over time), and artifact quantification via residual error map analysis. Expected outcomes: Our adaptive method should achieve 50–60 nm resolution at 30+ frames per second sustained over thousands of frames, significantly reducing photobleaching compared to full STED, and maintaining high tracking fidelity (localization precision <10 nm) for dynamic structures. Ablation studies: (i) fixed vs. adaptive update rate of diffusion prior, (ii) with and without spatiotemporal consistency constraint, (iii) uniform vs. dynamic undersampling mask generation, (iv) sensitivity to initialization and prior batch size. We will also test generalization on additional cell types (U2OS, primary neurons) without retraining.
